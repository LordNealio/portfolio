About

A lasting creative institution — not a single brand.

Just Neal is a Creative Director, Systems Designer, and Founder working across AI, luxury apparel, publishing, software, and education.

Through his creative house, NIL (Name. Image. Likeness.), he develops interconnected brands, products, and experiences that explore identity through design, technology, and storytelling. His multidisciplinary approach combines finance, global culture, software development, and visual communication into a body of work that favors timeless systems over fleeting trends — with the ambition of building a lasting creative institution rather than a single successful brand.

That range isn't scattered; it's the point. A background in finance and operations taught him how organizations actually run. Years of teaching taught him to make hard things clear. NIL is where those disciplines meet: one house, many rooms.

The backstory

How I became who I am

Life and death. Both have been my teachers. What I've lost and what I've lived through are the whole reason any of this exists.

Why I started

I was called to it — at a point when my life felt like it was falling apart, at work and beyond. The struggle wasn't a detour; it was the shaping. Every hard season sharpened the way I see, and that perspective is what I build from.

The moment that changed everything

Now. Not a date in the past — this one. The moment that changes everything is always the present one.

One house, many rooms

Fashion, AI, publishing, education, film, technology, and storytelling look like separate disciplines. Under NIL they're one practice.

Taking a name, an image, a likeness — and building something from nothing. I originate the concepts, direct the creative vision, and make the work, so that each artifact helps someone build themselves.

Featured Product · 2026 · Live

MindVault

A private thinking space that compounds over time

A local-first AI journaling app that turns daily reflection into habits, published ideas, and collective inquiry.

The problem. Journaling apps either lock your thoughts in a silo or send everything to a server. Reflection rarely turns into momentum — habits, ideas, or output.

A mobile-first app with a private local-first core and an opt-in AI layer. Every AI call is labeled at the point of use, so the private base stays private while an outbound layer can atomize goals into habits, publish ideas across formats, and host collective inquiry.

  • Daily journal with morning/evening flows, voice capture, and photo scan
  • Habit Atomizer — domain-aware questions genuinely re-rank suggested habits
  • Publish module — a private idea becomes an IG caption, LinkedIn post, X thread, newsletter, or blog outline
  • Throne Talk — a Socratic collective-inquiry mode with lenses, timed sessions, and AI synthesis

What I learnedThe hardest tension was keeping a private, local-first soul while adding an outbound AI/publishing layer. The answer was labeling every AI call at the point of use — and deferring anything platform-level until retention is proven.

PART III

A Black wHole

An interdisciplinary lab. The name holds absence and wholeness at the same time.

The Lab

An interdisciplinary lab

A Black wHole investigates language, identity, history, culture, money, science, memory, and loss — then transforms those inquiries into studies, public media, and practical tools.

The hole represents what has been erased, buried, fragmented, misunderstood, or lost. The whole represents what can begin to form when missing context is restored, separated ideas are brought back into relationship, and understanding is transformed into something useful. In that limited sense, it operates a little like a metaphysical CERN: ideas and disciplines are brought into contact instead of particles.

Navigating Ignorance

Ignorance is not always a lack of intelligence. It can be produced by missing information, inherited assumptions, restricted access, or erased history. This work navigates ignorance by restoring context. Find what is missing. Question what appears settled.

Gaining Glory

Glory is not celebrity. It is the recovery of memory, dignity, knowledge, creativity, confidence, ownership, and the ability to imagine beyond what has been inherited or assigned. Recover what was buried. Build from what is yours.

Especially Reparations

Understanding is incomplete if it never asks what should be repaired. Repair can involve money, land, ownership, access, preserved testimony, corrected records, education, and community infrastructure. Study the harm. Name what was taken. Build toward repair.

The studies

Two guided educational studies — part of the Black Builders Toolbox — measure how history, linguistics, and context shape perception. Each pairs a participant experience with an evidence-labeled education module: The Enigmatic Cipher and The Big Payback.

Honest statusA participant research experience, currently in Preview / Educational Demonstration Mode — no data is collected. Formal enrollment requires a backend and ethical / IRB review.

Essay One · RapGod

Dear Ye

It begins with a prayer

The story begins with a prayer. DMX's prayer for Kanye sets the tone — not with confidence, but with humility. Before any journey, there is surrender.

Then comes Kanye West: “I pray my feet don't fail me now…” The line carries obvious weight within Jesus Walks, but it also echoes a larger biblical pattern. Throughout Scripture, feet symbolize the journey of faith: Moses removes his sandals on holy ground; Isaiah speaks of the beautiful feet of those who bring good news; Jesus washes the disciples' feet; Revelation describes Christ's feet “like burnished bronze.”

Walking becomes more than movement. It becomes purpose.

What if your feet fail?

But my interpretation asks a different question. What if your feet do fail? What if the goal was never simply to walk?

The visual answers: “It's okay if your feet fail you… when you can fly.” That thought transitions into another DMX lyric: “The only thing I fear is that I'm never gonna fly.”

The story shifts from walking to flying. Then Kanye answers with Touch the Sky. The narrative becomes one of transcendence — not abandoning the journey, but rising above its limitations.

The Signals

From there, the soundtrack turns inward. In Remote Control Pt. 2, the lyric “Justin” catches my attention. In Chakras, I focus on the lyric “Neal when you see God,” which I interpret as another meaningful connection within the broader set of patterns I've been exploring.

Whether those connections are intentional or coincidental is for each listener to decide. For me, they become part of a larger artistic narrative.

Mission Control

Finally, everything arrives at Mission Control. Mission Control is where direction is given. Where communication begins. Where purpose becomes action.

The journey starts with a prayer that the feet won't fail. It ends with a mission. Not just to walk. Not just to fly. But to answer the call.

Essay Two · The Lab

I AM / 22

The elements, and the number 22

A Curious Observation

I AM, the elements, and the number 22

One of the recurring patterns I've explored is centered on the phrase “I AM.” Throughout Scripture, “I AM” carries profound theological significance as the name God reveals to Moses: “I AM WHO I AM.” — Exodus 3:14.

While studying language, symbolism, and science together, I noticed an unexpected numerical relationship involving the chemical elements represented by the letters I and Am. I is the chemical symbol for Iodine, whose atomic number is 53. Am is the chemical symbol for Americium, whose atomic number is 95.

Adding these atomic numbers together produces:
53 + 95 = 148
Reducing that sum to a single digit sequence:
1 + 4 + 8 = 13
Then reducing once more:
1 + 3 = 4

But another pattern stood out to me. Instead of focusing only on the total, I looked at the individual atomic numbers:
53 → 5 + 3 = 8
95 → 9 + 5 = 14 → 1 + 4 = 5
Adding those reduced values gives:
8 + 5 = 13 → 1 + 3 = 4

I also noticed another numerical relationship that became central to my broader theory:
53 − 31 = 22
95 − 73 = 22

This recurring appearance of 22 led me to investigate whether the number might have symbolic significance within the larger collection of patterns I was studying.

I AM / 22 · Plate

The Garment

I AM / 22 — the elements, the equation, the mass, the truth.

Why 22?

For me, the number 22 became interesting because it repeatedly appeared alongside themes involving identity, names, language, Scripture, and creation. I do not present this as scientific proof or established biblical doctrine. Rather, I view it as one observation among many that encouraged further exploration.

My work asks a broader question: can independent patterns drawn from different fields — language, mathematics, chemistry, history, and Scripture — sometimes converge in ways that invite deeper reflection?

Whether these convergences are meaningful or simply coincidental is something every reader should evaluate for themselves.

22 in the Kabbalah

The number surfaces again in a far older tradition. The Hebrew alphabet has twenty-two letters — and in Kabbalah, especially the Sefer Yetzirah, the “Book of Formation,” those twenty-two letters are described as the building blocks of creation: the elements through which the world was spoken into being.

On the Tree of Life, the ten sefirot — the divine emanations — are joined by twenty-two paths, and each path is traditionally assigned one of the twenty-two Hebrew letters. Twenty-two becomes the number of connection itself: the letters that link the hidden to the revealed, the “I Am” to everything it makes.

I do not present this as proof that elemental arithmetic and the Kabbalistic tradition describe the same thing. I offer it as a documented resonance — two very different systems in which twenty-two marks the letters, or the paths, of creation, gathered around a name.

I AM / 22 · Plate

Inniganus

“Begotten from the Darkness” (Genesis 1:2) · I Am — Iodine + Americium — “Indigo American” · 126 + 243 = 369 = 9.

An Invitation

This documents these observations openly and transparently. My goal is not to ask readers to accept any conclusion without question, but to present the patterns that caught my attention so others can examine them critically, test them independently, and decide for themselves what — if anything — they signify.

Curiosity has often led humanity to new discoveries. Sometimes a pattern is merely coincidence. Sometimes it points toward a deeper understanding. My hope is simply to encourage careful observation, thoughtful discussion, and an honest search for truth.

Essay Three · The Lab

Charm Quark × Big Ben

The Weight of “I Am”

A personal inquiry into physics, identity, Korea, Scripture, and the unexpected connections that travel across time.

The Question

Can a word have weight? Can a name carry a number? Can scientific history, personal history, language, chemistry, and Scripture intersect without becoming the same thing?

Charm Quark × Big Ben begins with those questions.

This project is not presented as a conventional scientific paper or as proof of a theological conclusion. It is a visual and intellectual research journal — a place where documented history is placed beside personal experience, symbolic interpretation, and open-ended hypotheses.

At its center are two parallel searches. Benjamin W. Lee helped physicists estimate the mass of the charm quark. I have been exploring what I symbolically call the mass of “I Am” — the possible weight, number, and elemental structure associated with the biblical name of God.

One belongs to established particle physics. The other is my personal framework of inquiry. The page begins where those two searches unexpectedly meet.

Benjamin W. Lee

A physicist between Korea and Ohio

Benjamin Whisoh Lee was a Korean American theoretical physicist whose work influenced the development of modern particle physics and the Standard Model.

Born in Seoul in 1935, Lee came to the United States as a student and earned his undergraduate degree from Miami University in Oxford, Ohio. He later studied at the University of Pittsburgh and the University of Pennsylvania before becoming one of the leading theoretical physicists of his generation.

Lee eventually led Fermilab's theoretical physics department from 1973 until his death in 1977. His career connected Korea, the American Midwest, and the expanding international world of high-energy physics. That geographic journey matters to this project — and so does the university where it began.

Miami University

A shared point of origin

Benjamin W. Lee attended Miami University. So did I.

I earned my bachelor's and master's degrees in accounting there. My field was not theoretical physics, but my education trained me to examine numbers, reconcile separate records, identify patterns, and ask why apparently disconnected values might belong to the same underlying system.

Discovering Lee's history changed how I understood my own relationship to Miami University. It was no longer only the place where I studied accounting; it was also part of the story of a Korean physicist who helped investigate one of matter's fundamental particles.

I do not claim that attending the same university establishes a scientific relationship between our work. I present it as the historical connection that inspired the inquiry.

The Charm Quark

Giving weight to the unseen

The charm quark is one of the six quark types recognized within the Standard Model of particle physics. Quarks cannot ordinarily be isolated and placed on a scale — their properties must be inferred through mathematics, experiments, particle interactions, decay patterns, and the larger particles they help form.

Benjamin W. Lee participated in theoretical work that estimated the charm quark's mass. This is what first captured my attention: before the charm quark could be directly understood, scientists had to reason toward it. They examined effects. They studied relationships. They used evidence to estimate the properties of something they could not simply hold in their hands.

The history of the charm quark becomes a model for asking careful questions about things that are real, influential, or meaningful but not immediately visible. That does not mean symbolic questions can be answered through particle physics — it means the scientific story inspired the structure of my search.

Big Ben

A name, a measure, a signal

“Big Ben” is my name for the Benjamin W. Lee dimension of this project. Ben points to Benjamin Lee. Big suggests scale, magnitude, significance, and mass. Big Ben, the famous clock, introduces time, measurement, repetition, and the striking of an hour.

Within the project, the phrase becomes a symbolic bridge between Benjamin Lee, scientific measurement, the mass of the charm quark, historical time, personal timing, numbers that recur, and the question of whether meaning can echo across generations.

This is not a claim that the landmark Big Ben has a documented relationship to Benjamin Lee or charm physics. It is wordplay used as a conceptual instrument — turning a scientific biography into a broader meditation on what humanity measures, and why.

“I Am”

A name that resists containment

In the biblical account of Moses and the burning bush, Moses asks what name he should give when the Israelites ask who sent him. God answers: “I AM WHO I AM.”

The phrase has been interpreted as a declaration of being, existence, presence, and divine self-sufficiency. “I Am” therefore enters this project not merely as an ordinary phrase, but as a name associated with existence itself. That produces the central symbolic question: if matter has mass, what would it mean to speak of the “mass” of I Am?

I am not claiming that God possesses a scientifically measurable particle mass. Instead, I use mass in several overlapping senses — physical mass, numerical weight, linguistic value, cultural significance, spiritual gravity, historical influence, and the emotional weight carried by a name. “The mass of I Am” is my name for this multidisciplinary investigation.

I + Am

Element, language, and identity

The phrase “I Am” can also be visually separated: I / Am. In chemistry, I is the symbol for iodine. Am is the symbol for americium. Placed together, “I Am” can be read simultaneously as a sentence of identity, a biblical declaration, and two symbols from the periodic table.

This does not make “I Am” a chemical formula, nor does it establish a new compound. It creates a symbolic structure — one that allows the words to be explored through several systems at once: language (I am), theology (I AM), chemistry (I + Am), mathematics (atomic numbers and masses), design (letters, blocks, numbers, grids, garments), and identity (the statement a person makes before describing who they are).

The project asks what becomes visible when those systems are placed beside one another without pretending that they are interchangeable.

22

Number as a point of inquiry

Within my research, 22 emerged as an important symbolic number associated with “I Am.” The number appears within a larger visual and mathematical framework involving elemental values, numerical reduction, identity, and the relationship between written symbols and measurable quantities. I refer to this framework as I AM / 22.

The number is not presented as the scientifically established “number of God.” It is the result of my own symbolic method and remains a hypothesis within the project. The more accurate statement is: I have developed a personal framework in which 22 represents the numerical weight of “I Am.”

That distinction matters. An observation can be documented. A calculation can be reproduced. An interpretation can be considered. A theological conclusion requires another level of reasoning.

The Rose of Sharon Blooms Again

When the story returns to Korea

Benjamin Lee's legacy extends beyond scientific journals. He also inspired the central physicist in the South Korean novel The Rose of Sharon Blooms Again, written by Kim Jin-myung and published in the 1990s. The book introduces another major symbol: the rose of Sharon.

In Korean, the flower is called mugunghwa — 무궁화 — widely regarded as the national flower of South Korea, its name conveying the idea of an eternal blossom that never fades. The flower carries ideas of endurance, renewal, national identity, survival, continuity, and blooming again after hardship.

Through the novel, Benjamin Lee becomes connected not only to Miami University and particle physics, but also to one of Korea's most recognizable national symbols. For me, that connection is especially meaningful because I also lived in Korea — a place where I taught, learned, and built relationships. Benjamin Lee → Korea → Miami University → physics → the rose of Sharon. My own line moves through Miami University → Korea → Scripture → identity → “I Am.” The project examines where those lines cross.

The Biblical Flower

“I am the rose of Sharon”

The phrase “rose of Sharon” also appears in the Bible. Song of Songs 2:1 is traditionally rendered: “I am the rose of Sharon, and the lily of the valleys.”

The identity of the original plant is uncertain; scholars have suggested the Hebrew term may refer to a crocus, narcissus, or another flowering plant rather than the modern flower now called the rose of Sharon. The South Korean mugunghwa and the ancient flower referenced in Song of Songs are not necessarily the same plant merely because English tradition uses the phrase for both. The connection is linguistic, cultural, and symbolic — not proof of botanical identity.

Yet the wording remains striking. The verse begins with the words: I am. That brings the flower directly into the central language of this project.

The Bloom

What does it mean to bloom again?

A flower that blooms again suggests return — not repetition in exactly the same form, but the reappearance of life after a period when it could not be seen.

Within Charm Quark × Big Ben, blooming again represents a forgotten history being recovered, a scientist's legacy being rediscovered, an idea reappearing in another discipline, a name gaining new meaning, Korea reentering a personal story, and something once hidden becoming visible.

Benjamin Lee's scientific work survives him. His image reappears in archives. His life reappears in fiction. The mugunghwa blooms again each season. A student walks the grounds of Miami University without knowing that another student from Korea once walked there before him. Years later, the connection blooms.

Observation, Interpretation, and Hypothesis

How to read this project

To keep the research transparent, every major idea should be understood through one of three categories.

Documented — information supported by historical records, scientific publications, recognized institutions, books, or primary documents. For example: Benjamin Lee attended Miami University; his research contributed to the scientific understanding of the charm quark; a Korean novel inspired by him was titled The Rose of Sharon Blooms Again; the mugunghwa is widely regarded as South Korea's national flower; the phrase “rose of Sharon” appears in traditional translations of Song of Songs 2:1.

Personal observation — a real feature of my own life or how I encountered the documented information. For example: I attended Miami University; I later lived and worked in Korea; I noticed the phrase “I am” within Song of Songs 2:1; I began examining “I” and “Am” as elemental symbols.

Symbolic interpretation — the meaning I draw from placing those facts and observations together: “Big Ben” as a symbol of measurement and time; “the mass of I Am” as a framework for spiritual and numerical weight; the rose blooming again as the return of an idea across generations; the number 22 as a proposed symbolic value.

Documented claims can be verified. Observations can be examined. Interpretations can be discussed. Hypotheses can be tested, refined, challenged, or rejected.

What I Am Not Claiming

This project does not claim that symbolic arithmetic replaces experimental science; that two people attending the same university proves a cosmic relationship; that the Korean mugunghwa is certainly the same species referenced in the Hebrew Bible; that wordplay constitutes scientific evidence; that a periodic-table reading of “I Am” creates a recognized chemical formula; or that a numerical pattern alone proves the identity, will, or physical properties of God.

These boundaries do not weaken the project. They make the real questions easier to see.

What I Am Asking

Why did Benjamin Lee's story resonate so strongly with me? What does it mean that our lives intersect through Miami University and Korea? Why does the language of mass become so powerful when applied to identity? Can a scientific discovery inspire a symbolic or theological investigation without being misrepresented? What happens when “I Am” is read as language, Scripture, chemistry, mathematics, and design? Why does the rose of Sharon appear at several points in the story? What is gained — and what is lost — when history is interpreted through personal symbolism? How can a person investigate meaningful patterns while remaining honest about uncertainty?

And finally: what is the weight of a name?

Final Statement

Benjamin W. Lee helped give scientists a way to think about the mass of the charm quark. His journey moved from Korea to Miami University and into the history of theoretical physics. His life later inspired a Korean novel named for a flower that blooms again. That flower is associated with Korea. Its English name also appears in a biblical sentence beginning with the words: I am.

I also attended Miami University. I also lived in Korea. And I have been exploring what I call the weight and number of “I Am.”

I do not present these intersections as final proof of a scientific or divine conclusion. I present them as the beginning of a disciplined question. A question about matter. A question about identity. A question about names. A question about what disappears. And a question about what — across time, countries, disciplines, and generations — may bloom again.

Document the facts. Follow the question. Name the difference. Let the rose bloom again.

PART VI

iNeed$

Modern patronage for independent ideas.

The invitation

Great work often exists because someone believed in it first.

Throughout history, artists, inventors, scientists, educators, and writers often reached their full potential because someone believed in them before the rest of the world did. This is a modern version of that tradition — for anyone who wants to help the work continue.

Most of the work here has been shared freely. Rather than placing everything behind a paywall, the aim is a model where people who believe in the work can help sustain it. Support is never expected. But it is deeply appreciated.

The honest framingContributions are personal gifts of support and patronage — not tax-deductible charitable donations.

PART VII

The Tithing Experiment

What if the world's wealthiest people tithed?

A thought experiment

What would happen if eight of the world's wealthiest people each gave 10%?

An interactive thought experiment — not a factual claim, legal demand, or real transfer of wealth — about generosity, patronage, and what extraordinary resources could make possible.

Many faith traditions have encouraged people to give a portion of what they have for purposes beyond themselves. The experiment asks a simple hypothetical: if the wealthiest chose to set aside 10%, where might the remaining 90% go? Visitors vote, then step into Spend Dat Shit to spend a hypothetical 90% — pairing absurd luxury with the real cost of housing, tuition, and repair.

“I only need one yes.”

Every invitation, every response, and every lesson is recorded in the open. No billionaire named has agreed to participate, endorsed the project, or transferred funds. Net-worth figures are illustrative estimates.

The math of one yes

If each of the Eight gave 10%.

A single voluntary tithe — ten percent — from each of the eight, against their illustrative estimated net worth:

Mark Zuckerberg$17.7B

Ten percent, given once, would endow this work with roughly $122.5 billion.

That is the scale the experiment is pointing at — enough to fund research, education, tools, and repair for generations. It remains a thought experiment built on public estimates; no one named has agreed to give anything. I only need one yes.
